About Triple Helix

Three institutions, one shared problem-solving process

Triple Helix is structured around three participating groups, each contributing a distinct role. This is a conceptual model for the prototype and will be refined as the initiative is formally structured.

Government

Identifies real-world challenges and administrative priorities, and provides problem statements for participants to work on.

Academia

Contributes student talent, research capability and mentorship from faculty to develop and refine proposed solutions.

Industry

Provides technical expertise, mentorship and technology support, and may offer pathways toward implementation or pilots.

Note: The Triple Helix model, participant roles and organisational structure described here are proposed for this prototype and are subject to official confirmation by the district administration.

Guidelines

Eligibility, team requirements and rules

Detailed official guidelines will be published closer to launch. The structure below shows what will be covered.

Who can participate

  • Eligibility criteria will be announced officially.
  • Potential participant categories may include students, college teams and academic institutions.
  • Cross-disciplinary participation is recommended, subject to confirmation.

Team requirements

  • Team size: To Be Announced
  • Institution requirements: To Be Announced
  • Cross-disciplinary teams: recommended, to be confirmed

Submission and conduct

  • Submission requirements will be published with official guidelines.
  • Use of external resources must be cited appropriately.
  • Intellectual property and disqualification conditions will be defined in official rules.

Do

  • Understand the problem statement clearly before building
  • Build a practical, working solution where possible
  • Cite any external resources or datasets used
  • Follow the submission requirements once published
  • Maintain ethical standards throughout

Don't

  • Submit copied or plagiarised work
  • Misrepresent third-party work as your own
  • Violate intellectual property rights
  • Ignore official submission rules once published
  • Use fabricated data in your submission

Indicative Evaluation Criteria — Subject to Official Guidelines

How submissions may be assessed

These criteria are indicative and intended to guide preparation. Official evaluation criteria will be published with the guidelines.

Problem Understanding

How clearly the submission addresses the stated problem.

Innovation

Originality of the proposed approach.

Technical Approach

Soundness of the technical implementation.

Feasibility

Practicality of building and deploying the solution.

Impact

Potential benefit to the intended users or community.

Scalability

Whether the approach could extend beyond the initial scope.

Presentation

Clarity of the final presentation to the evaluation panel.
